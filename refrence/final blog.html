AI assistants have changed how customers find businesses. In 2026, nearly half of buying journeys start with a question to ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity — not a Google search. And when the AI answers, it doesn't list ten links. It names two or three brands.

We wanted to know why some small businesses show up in those answers while others — often with better products — are completely ignored. So our team analysed 4,200 AI answers across 12 industries and tested changes on 18 client brands over 120 days.

The paradox: great businesses, invisible to AI

Many of the businesses we studied had excellent reviews, strong Google rankings, and loyal customers. Yet when we asked AI assistants for recommendations in their category, they didn't appear at all.

The reason is simple once you see it: AI assistants don't rank pages — they recommend brands they understand and trust. A business can rank #1 on Google and still be invisible to ChatGPT if the AI can't clearly tell what it does, where it operates, and who vouches for it.

That's the paradox. Traditional SEO success doesn't automatically translate to AI visibility.

What gets you cited by AI assistants

After testing seven tactics, four made a clear, measurable difference. These are structural changes, not quick tricks.

1. Answer-first pages

Pages that open with a clear, 40–60 word answer — what you do, who for, where, and at what price range — were cited far more often. AI can lift that answer straight into its response.

2. Consistent brand facts everywhere

When your business name, location, services, and founding year match across your website, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, and directories, AI treats you as a reliable entity. Conflicting details make it hesitate.

3. Third-party mentions

Around 71% of citations came from sites other than the brand's own — review platforms, "best of" lists, and news articles. What others say about you matters more than what you say about yourself.

4. Recent, genuine reviews

For local and service searches, businesses with 50+ reviews in the last 12 months were significantly more likely to be recommended.

What gets you ignored: 5 common patterns

The same patterns appeared again and again in businesses AI assistants skipped over.

Vague service pages

Pages full of phrases like "we deliver innovative solutions" give AI nothing to quote. If a page doesn't clearly say what you do and for whom, it won't be used as a source.

Conflicting business information

Different addresses, old phone numbers, or mismatched service lists across the web confuse AI models. When in doubt, they recommend someone else.

No presence beyond your own website

If the only place your business is described is your own site, AI has no independent confirmation that you're trustworthy.

Keyword-stuffed "AI pages"

Pages written to game AI with repeated phrases performed slightly worse. Modern models reward clarity and corroboration, not repetition.

Outdated content

Pages with no visible update date and stale information were cited less, especially by Perplexity, which favours fresh sources.

The real difference: how AI decides who to trust

AI visibility isn't decided by one factor. It comes down to how clearly and consistently your business is described — by you and by others. Think of it in three levels:

  • Low visibility: AI knows little about you, or finds conflicting information. You're rarely mentioned.
  • Medium visibility: Your website is clear, but few other sources confirm it. You're mentioned occasionally.
  • High visibility: Your facts are consistent everywhere, and trusted third parties recommend you. You're named regularly.

Our finding: Most small businesses sit at "medium" — their own website is fine, but the rest of the web says little about them. That's the biggest opportunity.

WHAT'S NEXT

Where AI search is headed in 2027

AI assistants are starting to take actions — booking appointments, requesting quotes, and comparing prices on the user's behalf. Businesses with structured, machine-readable information will be the ones these AI agents can actually work with.

Getting your brand facts and service details in order now isn't just about being mentioned. It's about being usable by the next generation of AI tools.

What to do before investing in AI search

Before spending on AI search services or tools, get these basics in place. They cost little and make every other effort work better.

Audit your brand facts

List where your business appears online and check that your name, address, phone number, services, and description match everywhere.

Rewrite your top 5 pages answer-first

Start with your homepage and key service pages. Open each with a short, direct answer to the main question customers ask.

Track a fixed set of prompts

Write down 20–30 questions customers might ask AI about your category, and check them monthly across ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity. Download our free prompt tracking template.

Build steady third-party mentions

  • Ask happy customers for reviews every month.
  • Get listed in trusted industry directories and "best of" roundups.
  • Share expert commentary with local and trade publications.
